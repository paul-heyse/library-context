# Holistic state management: semantic authority, live ownership and execution fit

**Design review · tier design · purpose target · 2026-10-10**

**Decision: Revise.** This review concerns all stored and active state needed to compile, publish, serve, inspect, recover and maintain the API and evidence product. It does not accept the enclosing architecture or qualify a release. The principal reviewer formed the judgment independently from current owners and decisive source, with bounded persistent-state and memory-state investigations and coordinator-supplied receipt/library evidence.

The architecture has a substantial foundation worth retaining: exact immutable native views, distinct nominal and content identities, model-owned semantics, checked admission, current provenance on reuse, independent cold admission, pinned executable definitions and conservative protection of uncertain effects. These are more than documentation conventions. The executing paths use them to refuse incompatible work. Shared persistence is a sound candidate realization, and nothing found here establishes that a universal graph manager, generic state service or new workflow engine would improve it.

The assembled system nevertheless remains inadequate for its supported state lifecycles. Some consequences of the domain model are reconstructed independently in transport and recovery consumers. Some operations select an exact semantic universe but materialize that entire universe in server arrays. Some locally bounded transitions contain unbounded child effects or hold broad coordination through expensive work. Live derived representations also outlast their actual consumers or bypass the operation's existing resource owner. Most seriously, an audit can release its publication pin and invalidate its client while compiler-owned background transport drainage remains outstanding. These are related through ownership and operation composition, but they are not one defect and do not have one replacement mechanism.

The recent failures are useful entry points, not the scope boundary. The 08:21 UTC native browse journey failed during final reconciliation with a 1 MiB `array::concat` error; a separate catalog journey completed reconciliation and publication readback, then returned ResourceRefused where UnknownLibrary was expected. A small-publication retirement failed with a 10 s transaction timeout. The precise offending reconciliation statement and the cause of the UnknownLibrary/ResourceRefused difference remain unresolved. Source independently establishes complete-array preparation, transaction amplification and lifecycle defects; the review does not claim that these diagnoses explain every observed delay or failure.

## 3. Governing contracts and composed operations

The semantic dependency direction remains model definitions → specialized realizations. Core coordinates compilation and admission, native storage realizes guarded persistent state and publisher exposes immutable manifests. Serving depends on model/native contracts, without acquiring compiler authority. Filesystem storage delegates native retirement. That direction is sound.

The critical contracts are verbs, not merely named record shapes:

- **Select a completed domain:** consume exact views, declared root/role/prefix intent and fresh membership; produce selected immutable premises with explicit missing/empty outcomes. Later insertions, deletions and changed coverage alter the premise. Ordinary selected discovery remains separate from complete integrity inventory.
- **Reuse:** consume complete compatible dependencies and current producer specification; produce a pure value or a checked current attachment. Hash equality never creates semantic authority. A cached pure value cannot restore runtime grants or old provenance.
- **Publish/read:** consume admitted state and trusted installed definitions; expose one immutable manifest, with fresh runtime pins for actual consumers. Visibility and operator selection remain separate. Streamed rows remain provisional until required statement terminals and physical EOF.
- **Retire:** consume exact queue/object identity, current guard/incoming holds and original authorization epoch; durably preserve child discoverability before releasing a parent. Neither mtime nor local process exit establishes eligibility.
- **Transport/restore:** select the complete logical content closure, normalize runtime ownership only for data transport, stage fresh ownership and independently admit actual rows. Imported executable/control records confer no authority.
- **Close an operation:** fence new entry, join every owned descendant's actual terminal result, retain pin/client/resources through that join, report primary and finalization failures, then release authority. This contract is implemented well in several places but is missing at the audit/compiler composition.

For analysis records, the scope remains explicit:

| Question/stage | Projection and method | Fidelity/model, resources and output |
|---|---|---|
| Scope discovery/normalization | Model relation-scope programs; selected roots separate from full owned/negative domains; native/relational/finite lowerings | Exact-under-contract selection; typed shared premises feed fresh predicates. Complete absent/missing outcomes and current source identity remain. |
| Invocation/Summary/Structural | Independent vertex inventory, identity-bearing directed arcs and declared simplification; petgraph topology and canonical callee-first SCC order | Exact graph result or model-qualified summary as declared; charged graph and SCC workspace; evidence/semantic IDs remain outside local indices. |
| Optional analytics | Declared projection/method/settings; retained required universe | Heuristic outputs stay governed and do not become behavioral proof. Complete versus limited/refused work remains explicit. |
| Search/brief/evidence | Exact eligible scope before quotas, separately declared ranking channels and retained ordering | Model fidelity and original evidence pin remain; server-side full arrays and uncharged retained ranking are execution/resource defects, not permission to shrink the semantic universe. |

The independent cold checks remain necessary. Sharing a captured immutable selection within one audit is different from trusting a dump's claimed closure. Sharing closure definitions is different from deriving the independent semantic oracle from the same produced bytes. These distinctions constrain every recommendation below.

Immutable product identity also explains why a generic invalidation broadcast is not needed for the examined consumers. Whole-stage requests bind actual resolved prefixes and exact completed-view identities; selected products perform fresh complete membership/content-token/root-outcome discovery before lookup. A later insertion, deletion or coverage change produces a different premise identity while an old pinned input remains valid for its old consumer. Separately, `attach_retained_product` checks full ContributionSpec/exact inputs, rechecks admitted completed source state transactionally and creates current attempt ownership (`compiler.rs:1288-1386`). An immutable value hit and a fresh runtime capability are different products of these operations. The review found no demonstrated invalidation gap in those routes.

<a id="HS-F01"></a>
### HS-F01 — Exact state closure has several independent interpreters

**Diagnosis: Implemented source establishes duplicated semantic decisions; remedy: Proposed.** FP-01/02/04/06, DP-01/06/08/16/24, A1–A3, G1. The native owner has six `STATE_TABLES` (`compiler.rs:5266-5273`) and a separate `state_query` match expressing ownership/view/node/alias selection (`:963-991`). Restore duplicates the six-table inventory (`restore.rs:26-33`), builds its own owner/view/node/original/binding sets (`:395-405`) and independently interprets per-family closure (`:678-703`). Import has its own data/derived classification (`backup_import.rs:19-46`). Different ingress policies are legitimate; independently deciding which durable relations constitute one complete content recovery closure is not merely codec variation.

Adding a backing family requires hidden coordination across these consumers. A model declaration and valid native completion do not govern transport completeness automatically. Today’s listed families can be correct while the extension boundary remains defective. A newly required family can be omitted, handled as an unknown forbidden table, or restored with a different owner relationship. This is the bounded A2 gap; the record/identity model is otherwise strong.

**Correction boundary.** Establish one small owned operation/declaration for the recoverable content closure: what it roots, which typed relation kinds it follows, which complete-empty/optional outcomes matter, which derived forms rebuild and which runtime/security state is excluded. Native selected access and portable lowering consume that definition, with their physical ordering/encoding retained. Separate independent cold inventory and corruption checks remain. This can be an ordinary native/model module; no universal state schema or generic walker is required. Delete duplicated closure interpretation as consumers migrate, not their independent ingress validators.

**Alternative/countercase.** A new derived index may deliberately be excluded and rebuilt, while a new canonical original backing family must be included. A blanket “all tables” generator would lose that distinction and might import runtime grants. The contract must express the distinction, not just consolidate strings. Keeping separate complete implementations is viable only if they are explicitly qualified independent implementations of one authoritative operation rather than separately evolving meanings; that carries continued extension cost.

**Closure evidence.** A revealing new backing-family extension reaches completion, hash/export, selected backup and fresh restore through one meaning owner; omission, extra selected rows, originals and hostile runtime records still fail independently. Inspect affected native/transport dependencies and remove obsolete interpretation branches. Full production repair is not authorized by this review.

### HS-F02 — Exact selection still creates whole-universe arrays before streaming

**Diagnosis: Implemented structure and an attributed failed journey; remedy: Proposed.** FP-03/07, DP-08/10/20, CI-07/08, A4. `derived_search.rs:46-61` gathers every selected node and alias into concatenated/distinct arrays; `:86-90` gathers all selected lexical/vector occurrences and document/vector IDs; `:158-170` concatenates all four result families before ordering. `reconciliation.rs:205-214` separately gathers all selected participant/reference IDs and concatenates them to find external targets. The compiler state's selected-node preparation also uses a complete array (`compiler.rs:971`). These are exact scopes, but exactness is not bounded physical preparation.

A larger publication or high-degree owner can overflow the server's array allowance before any output limit or client stream helps. The browse receipt demonstrates this error class during final reconciliation, but does not locate the exact expression. Existing indexed point restrictions are valuable corrections; they do not make a complete preliminary ID array selective or spillable.

**Correction boundary.** Carry exact view/owner/root intent into indexed candidate selection; use row/byte-bounded pointer windows, projection, independent cursors and bounded ordering/merge/dedup at the owner capable of it. Compare indexed native joins, one-owner streams and native/DF/Rust merge as complete operations. Full integrity reconciliation must still nominate **all actual** selected roles/occurrences—including unexpected IDs, fields and extras—rather than filter by expected IDs. Search must apply exact eligibility before channel limits.

**Alternative/countercase.** A global integrity check legitimately needs every selected role, and a negative answer may need complete absence proof. Limiting it to requested output or applying LIMIT before eligibility would make the answer cheaper by changing its meaning. A compact reused exact resolver can help broad repeated demand, but forcing sparse requests to build a whole-view cache recreates startup amplification.

**Closure evidence.** Inspect actual query structure and the pinned planner for the selected route; show complete mixed-family/skew/empty results beyond the current array threshold, foreign-view exclusion, unexpected-row refusal, dedup/order and all statement terminals/EOF. <a id="HS-F03"></a>
### HS-F03 — Retirement bounds candidates, not the full atomic effect

**Diagnosis: Implemented structure; failed retirement receipt corroborates the boundary, without primitive attribution. Remedy: Proposed.** FP-02/07, DP-08/19/20, A4; G5 remains constrained by exact recovery obligations. `prepare_retirement_children` (`control.rs:939-988`) already streams each indexed owner's children, checks terminals/EOF and durably enqueues before releasing a parent. Preserve that. `finish_retirement_window` (`:990-1004`) then holds installation coordination while a FOR loop performs guard reads/updates, incoming-hold checks, object deletion, full outgoing-hold selection/deletion and queue/job updates for up to 128 nominated objects (`:1019-1034`). One high-degree parent's outgoing holds are not bounded by the candidate count. A bounded window can therefore contain a large transaction and contend with unrelated ownership transitions.

**Correction boundary.** Native lifecycle owns eligibility, reference guards, late-write fences and durable progress. Choose atomic units by the actual protected effects, separating child-edge retirement/progress where semantics permit and using bulk/indexed operations where they reduce work. Narrow installation coordination to the epoch/admission/backup invariant it actually protects; retain exact object guards. Do not “repair” this by increasing healthy-command timeouts or reducing test/compiler parallelism.

**Alternative/countercase.** One atomic parent deletion with all outgoing holds is simple and may be best for low degree. Splitting it must not release the parent before children are durable, allow a concurrent hold to be missed, reset an earlier operation's epoch or strand the remaining subtree. Per-object transitions or resumable edge pages are viable only with those premises; batching many whole subtrees together is not automatically better.

**Closure evidence.** High-degree parent and many low-degree objects; concurrent late hold and same-content reactivation; interruption between child enqueue, hold deletion and parent release; unknown acknowledgement reconciliation; unchanged pinned subtrees. Source/query inspection must establish bounded effect units and actual access paths. The existing 10 s failure remains a failed receipt until the matching-source case passes.

### HS-F04 — Logical backup locality ends after whole-database export

**Diagnosis: Implemented and explicitly documented; remedy: Proposed.** FP-02/07, DP-08/10/19, A4. `backup.rs:52-65` exports the entire configured database into an engine spool, then `restore::compact_export` decodes/prepares and narrows to the requested closure (`restore.rs:821-865`). The requested publication pin is retained through finalization (`backup.rs:89-100`), and one engine read snapshot supplies coherence. Those guarantees are strengths. The unavoidable current physical input nevertheless includes unrelated retained publications, validation residue and operational history. A small exact backup grows with the shared database, including network/spool/disk work before narrowing.

**Correction boundary.** Compare a selected closed-content exporter under a coherent native read snapshot/transaction with the current engine dump and with immutable closed segments carrying a genuine recovery consumer. Produce the same complete logical data, definition metadata and original closure, with checked terminals and atomic output publication. Whole-service security/coordination recovery remains separate. The selected exporter is not yet qualified; the finding does not authorize replacing the current route with inconsistent paged reads.

**Alternative/countercase.** A whole-service cold recovery must preserve installation/security/runtime recovery assets and may legitimately require the whole store. It must not be narrowed to one content manifest. Conversely, independently paging current rows for logical backup can mix retirement/publication moments despite a correct final manifest. If a snapshot-scoped selected route cannot be exposed safely at the pinned engine, retain the whole snapshot route as an explicit bounded product restriction and revisit the physical packaging decision rather than silently claiming selected I/O.

**Closure evidence.** Selected backup with much unrelated retained content, concurrent publication/retirement, original and prerequisite omissions, multi-publication selection, fresh data-only restore, successful terminal export and output durability failure handling. <a id="HS-F05"></a>
### HS-F05 — Native operational history has safety authority but no complete growth lifecycle

**Diagnosis: Implemented retained history and unindexed live-state queries; remedy: Proposed.** FP-04/07, DP-04/08/19/21, A2/A4. `control.rs:108-115` declares effects, attempts, pins, guards and retirement progress. Each effect allocates a fresh operation and later marks it resolved (`:213-243,276`); pin release marks released (`:544`); attempt closure releases holds but retains its terminal row (`:547-624`); retirement leaves done queue state. `check_pending_effects`/`drain_effects` query `resolved=false` with LIMIT1/128 (`:332-373`), but the schema defines no resolved-state index. Native maintenance fences/drains/checks state (`compiler.rs:680-711`); it does not supply terminal-history compaction.

The search covered native source, publisher and relevant service/storage scripts. It found no explicit terminal control-history collector or complete compaction contract. This is a bounded absence claim, not a claim that every row is disposable. Unfinished operations, referenced attempts, unresolved pins/cleanup and retired-through watermarks retain real authority. With a long-lived service, safety state accumulates and empty-live-set observation can process growing terminal history. Whole-database export compounds that cost.

**Correction boundary.** Native ownership should define terminal record classes, required replay/acknowledgement horizons, references and permanent compact fencing authority. Qualify indexed live subsets and an explicit compaction operation where safe. Preserve old-operation refusal through a compact permanent watermark or equivalent mechanism before removing rich records. Reuse the existing reachability/control owner; filesystem lifecycle must continue to delegate.

**Alternative/countercase.** Age-based deletion of a resolved effect can let a delayed original transaction recreate or reinterpret an identity after its fence disappears. A retired object can later be legitimately recreated with identical content; the older authorization must still remain excluded. Pure indexing can repair empty-live scans without solving storage growth and may be the first sufficient correction; compaction needs additional semantic design, not a blanket TTL.

**Closure evidence.** Repeated operations with large terminal history and few live records; explicit retained references; lost acknowledgement and pre-intent fence; delayed original execution after compaction; same-content reactivation; interrupted collector restart. Identify which metadata is deliberately permanent and why. Do not close this finding with a size/age inventory alone.

**Alternative/countercase.** An imported descriptor claiming empty membership cannot be trusted as an already checked selection. ### HS-F07 — Compiler graph/binding preparations outlive their last useful stage

**Diagnosis: Implemented lifetimes; remedy: Proposed.** FP-01/07, DP-10/19/20, A4. `compilation.rs:803-814,853-867` creates a union of required graph preparations in outer driver scope. Summary/Structural/Analytic consume it (`:998-1024`); later coverage, synthesis, retrieval and final completion continue through `:1076` while `graphs` remains live. **Correction boundary.** Core's driver should own last-consumer lifetimes, including cache-hit branches and optional/deselected analytics. <a id="HS-F08"></a>
### HS-F08 — Ranked retention and replay do not compose with the existing resource owner

**Diagnosis: Implemented ownership/lock structure; remedy: Proposed.** FP-01/03/07, DP-08/10/19/20, A1/A3/A4. NativeService creates a shared pool and scoped PreparedCache but initializes `RankedResults::default()` (`service.rs:27-47`). Ranked state has its own count/byte/expiry counter and raw byte vectors (`ranked_results.rs:14-38`). Page construction serializes the complete retained ranking before shared-map insertion/make_room (`:162-211`). Concurrent requests can therefore hold construction copies alongside old retained entries outside the shared/request reservation contract. Retention accounting is not a reservation for active scratch or live borrowers.

Replay holds the map mutex through vector/cursor checks, template and row decoding, response assembly/serialization and decode validation (`:355-435`). This serializes unrelated retained results and ties eviction ownership to substantial replay work. `attach` parses/encodes before taking the map lock (`:284-309`); this review does not misattribute that work to the lock-held region. No observed OOM or quantified capacity claim is made.

**Correction boundary.** Charge retained bytes and construction/replay scratch through the existing selected service/request pool, retaining the current meaningful ranking policy. Borrow an immutable charged entry under a short map lock; perform replay outside that lock. Eviction removes optional map retention but must not release a live borrower's charge or publication authority. Join outstanding request/borrow ownership during close using existing lifecycle patterns, without forcing the ranking store into the preparation cache's exact mechanism.

**Alternative/countercase.** A continuation already issued to a borrower remains valid while another request evicts map retention; the borrower must retain its immutable bytes until replay ends. Reusing Moka for all state would not automatically supply this guarantee or preserve current session/result/digest policy. Streaming a page from a retained immutable ranking is viable; rediscovering search or embedding is not equivalent continuation behavior.

**Closure evidence.** Concurrent ranking construction, pressure/eviction with live replay, expiry, changed channels/snapshot/request/order and close during replay. Show existing composed budget charges remain live for scratch/borrowers and expensive conversion runs outside the map lock. Preserve explicit unavailable continuation outcomes and independent final-envelope admission.

<a id="HS-F09"></a>
### HS-F09 — Continuation replay replaces selected response policy with a hidden default

**Diagnosis: Implemented, with a source-revealed legitimate countercase; remedy: Proposed local correction.** FP-02/04/06, DP-01/05/08, A2/A3, G1/G6. `ranked_results.rs:428-436` calls `decode_response` with `ResourceLimits::default()` instead of the NativeService's selected limits. `serving/dispatch.rs:71-75` immediately checks the raw response against that object's `response_bytes`. Selected ResourceLimits permit a default allowance above 32 KiB (`serving/resources.rs:7-75`). With a configured 128 KiB ordinary allowance and expanded allowance at least that large, a legitimate 40 KiB structured replay and its actual final MCP envelope can fit the selected policy but be rejected by the hidden 32 KiB default before selected final packing/encoding applies. The larger allowance avoids confusing this countercase with the final envelope's text/structured duplication. This reasoning was not executed as a test.

**Correction boundary.** Consume the selected policy at replay decoding, or separate pure structural decoding from the one authoritative byte-admission operation. Keep final MCP-envelope admission and cursor binding independent. No new resource limits or cap increases are recommended.

**Alternative/countercase.** A selected allowance smaller than the default must still be enforced at the promised final boundary; simply removing every check could admit invalid output. A structurally valid retained page that the final packer can legally fit must not be rejected by an unrelated default first. The remedy should preserve both cases with one explicit policy source.

**Closure evidence.** Configured larger/smaller ordinary and expanded allowances, valid multirow continuation, final-envelope overhead and indivisible-core refusal. A focused regression at this boundary is sufficient; no provider or database campaign is needed to demonstrate the default-policy defect.

<a id="HS-F10"></a>
### HS-F10 — Audit can finalize the reader while its compiler stream still drains

**Diagnosis: Implemented ownership seam, not a reproduced runtime race; remedy: Proposed.** FP-02/03/04/06, DP-08/19/20/21, A2/A3, G5. Audit constructs NativeCompilerStore with `reader.shared_client()` (`inspection.rs:179-186`). `verify_state_inner` can return early on semantic validation failure while its backing CompilerRows remains unexhausted (`compiler.rs:3417-3421`). Dropping CompilerRows spawns asynchronous drainage holding the store/lease and records late errors in the store (`:4819-4856`). `track_rows` retains the compiler store, not the NativeReader ReaderScope/pin (`:2255-2265`); raw NativeRows has no such scope supplied in that path.

The compiler is local to the audit's inner block. Audit then calls `reader.close()` and invalidates the shared client (`inspection.rs:207-214`) without joining `compiler.drain_report()`. NativeReader close only checks its ReaderScope Arc count before releasing its pin (`reader.rs:45-55`); that count cannot observe the separate compiler descendant. Thus the promised terminal ownership composition is incomplete: pin/client release may race the tail drain, and the returned Completion can omit a late secondary transport failure. Conservative durable state does not make the returned operation's terminal claim complete.

**Correction boundary.** Audit retains its read-only compiler owner through explicit terminal drainage, folds its structured terminal/secondary outcomes into Completion, and only then releases reader pin and invalidates the client. Construction failure and every semantic early exit need the same order. Do not abandon an unrelated attempt or use read-only audit as a maintenance operation. An owned reader scope passed into compiler streams is an alternative if it truly integrates both pin lifetime and error collection; merely increasing Arc counts is insufficient.

**Alternative/countercase.** On a valid audit with all streams naturally exhausted, explicit finalization should be cheap and idempotent. On a corrupt first row with a delayed trailing error, preserving the useful semantic failure must not hide the terminal error or release the pin early. Drop-time cleanup remains valuable for exceptional unwinding; replacing it with immediate cancellation without confirmed finality would weaken the guarantee.

**Closure evidence.** Early corrupt backing plus controlled delayed physical EOF/late error; audit retains publication pin/client until drain finishes and reports primary and secondary failures. Include successful/idempotent close and construction failure. The isolated capture-sharing patch does not establish this correction.
